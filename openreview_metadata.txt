Joint Training with Machine-Generated Text Detection Can Distort Sarcasm Detection: Diagnosing Class-Conditional Negative Transfer


================================================================================
【2】ABSTRACT  摘要（192 词，上限 200）
================================================================================

Sarcasm detection requires intent beyond literal wording. Large language models (LLMs) can expand sarcasm resources, but evaluation must distinguish sarcasm-relevant pragmatic cues from source-related ones. A shared encoder supports both sarcasm detection and machine-generated text detection (MGTD), yet source-predictive cues may distort sarcasm predictions. We harmonize Chinese and English resources and construct DualTest for source × sarcasm subgroup analysis, with prompt-specified style serving as the sarcasm label for AI-generated texts. On AI-generated texts prompted to be non-sarcastic, the final plain joint model's false-positive rate exceeds that of its sarcasm-only counterpart by 67.0 percentage points, a pattern we call class-conditional negative transfer. Scores shift upward despite substantial within-AI discrimination. Both languages show the pattern, which persists when unchanged detectors evaluate a second test set generated by another LLM. Representation diagnostics link source-related scores and directions to the affected sarcasm decisions and show that removing one data-derived high-variance direction changes the subgroup gap. Exploratory analysis finds that predicted human-authorship probabilities do not consistently track LLM-judge ratings of human-likeness obtained without ground-truth source labels. These findings motivate source × sarcasm subgroup evaluation and external validation before detector probabilities are reused as quality scores or rewards.

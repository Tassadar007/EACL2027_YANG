\paragraph{A.3 Data quality checks.} All automated checks pass: language balance, label balance, balance across five folds, 99.97\% completeness of human--AI pairing (9{,}996 / 9{,}999 pairs), and chain-of-thought \citep{wei2022cot} leakage detection. Train--test overlap comprises two SarcTrain instances (0.02\%) and five AuthTrain instances (0.025\%), all on the AI side of the test set; overlap on the human subset is zero. SarcTrain's text-duplication rate is 0.34\% for English / 2.16\% for Chinese. Chinese duplication is concentrated in the sarcastic bucket (4.24\%; non-sarcastic bucket: 0.08\%) and results from the oversampling used to fill that bucket (A.1).

\phantomsection\label{app:a4}\paragraph{A.4 AI-text generation protocol.} All AI texts are produced by locally deployed generation models through the Ollama API (deepseek-r1:8b for the main results; qwen3.5:9b for cross-generator replication), using temperature $= 0.8$, top-$p = 0.95$ \citep{holtzman2020nucleus}, and a generation limit of 500 tokens. Generation instructions use four templates defined by language $\times$ style, with style determined by the seed instance's sarcasm label. The templates explicitly constrain output style and length (20--80 Chinese characters; 10--50 English words); the length constraint also mitigates the confound of distinguishing human and AI texts by length (see the length-controlled analysis in \Appref{app:repr}). The complete Chinese sarcastic template used in the experiment is reproduced verbatim below:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
【任务】生成一条讽刺风格的评论\\
【主题】\{seed\}\\
【要求】\\
- 直接给出简短的评论，不要解释思路或分析\\
- 风格要讽刺、幽默、夸张\\
- 表达与主题相关的观点即可，不必追求严谨\\
- 20-80字为佳\\
【评论】
\end{quote}

Its pinyin transliteration is:

\begin{quote}\small
{[}rènwù{]} shēngchéng yī tiáo fěngcì fēnggé de pínglùn\\
{[}zhǔtí{]} \{seed\}\\
{[}yāoqiú{]}\\
- zhíjiē gěichū jiǎnduǎn de pínglùn, bùyào jiěshì sīlù huò fēnxī\\
- fēnggé yào fěngcì, yōumò, kuāzhāng\\
- biǎodá yǔ zhǔtí xiāngguān de guāndiǎn jíkě, bùbì zhuīqiú yánjǐn\\
- 20--80 zì wéi jiā\\
{[}pínglùn{]}
\end{quote}

Its English translation is:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
[Task] Generate a sarcastic-style comment\\
{[Topic]} \{seed\}\\
{[Requirements]}\\
- Directly provide a short comment; do not explain your reasoning or analysis\\
- The style should be sarcastic, humorous, and exaggerated\\
- It is sufficient to express a viewpoint related to the topic; strict rigor is not required\\
- Preferably 20--80 Chinese characters\\
{[Comment]}
\end{quote}

The Chinese non-sarcastic version changes only the style line to ``The style should be normal and natural.'' The two English templates parallel the Chinese templates (the style lines are ``sarcastic, humorous, exaggerated'' and ``normal, natural,'' respectively; length constraint: 10--50 words). Post-processing comprises three steps: removing \texttt{<think>\dots</think>} chain-of-thought segments; removing reasoning-preface terms such as ``好的'' (\textit{hǎo de} `okay'), ``嗯'' (\textit{ń} `hmm'), ``Okay,'' ``Let me,'' and ``First''; and removing Markdown markup. Failed generations are discarded. AuthTrain contains three generation failures (9{,}996 complete pairs; the corresponding human instances are retained). The AI side of the main DualTest set likewise excludes a small number of failures; cross-generator replication has zero failures (hence 1{,}550 AI $\times$ non-sarcastic instances for the replication set versus 1{,}513 for the main set). For cross-generator replication, thinking mode is explicitly disabled for the reasoning model (Ollama \texttt{think = false}) to prevent the reasoning process from exhausting the output budget. Inline chain-of-thought from deepseek-r1:8b is removed through the post-processing described above.

\paragraph{A.5 SFT training and generation-evaluation set.} GenTrain (2{,}428; 1{,}214 each in Chinese and English) is constructed by sampling approximately 40\% of positive-sarcasm instances suitable for generation tasks and oversampling the minority language to achieve balance. By prompt type, it comprises 374 rewriting, 839 response, and 1{,}215 comment instances. Generation evaluation uses an independent set of 300 prompts (180 Chinese and 120 English): the Chinese task is to generate a sarcastic comment on a given topic, and the English task is to rewrite a normal sentence as a sarcastic one. The detector scoring and three blinded LLM evaluations in \Sref{sec:eval} are both conducted on this set.

\paragraph{B.2 Shared training hyperparameters.} Maximum 10 epochs; 200 warmup steps; weight decay 0.01; early-stopping patience 5 (normalized joint validation loss: $0.5 \times$ [sarcasm validation loss / first-epoch value] $+ 0.5 \times$ [authorship validation loss / first-epoch value]); gradient clipping 1.0; physical batch size $4 \times$ gradient accumulation $4 =$ effective batch size 16; sampling ratio authorship:sarcasm $= 2$:1; sarcasm class weights [1.0, 2.5] (both the class weights and sampling ratio were specified a priori and not tuned on the test set); seed 42 (reinitialization with seed $+$ fold index for each fold; all architecture/ablation experiments share the fold assignments and seed scheme); fixed 90/10 authorship-data holdout shared across all folds. Final model: retrained on all of SarcTrain and all of AuthTrain for a number of epochs equal to the mode of the best early-stopping epochs across the five runs $+1$ (maximum 10), with an independent seed (seed $+ 1000$), no validation set, and no early stopping. All main tables report the five-run mean $\pm$ standard deviation rather than a point estimate from the final model.

\paragraph{Software and hardware environment.} Detector training and inference are performed on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 (24\,GB); PyTorch 2.9.0 (CUDA 12.8), transformers 4.57.1, and peft 0.18.0. AI texts are generated locally through Ollama (\hyperref[app:a4]{Appendix~A.4}); blinded LLM evaluation uses API calls made in May 2026 (\hyperref[app:c3]{Appendix~C.3}).

\paragraph{B.3 SFT (QLoRA).} Qwen3-8B; 4-bit loading (bfloat16 computation); LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} rank 64 / alpha 16 / dropout 0; target modules q/k/v/o\_proj and gate/up/down\_proj; maximum sequence length 2{,}048; gradient checkpointing enabled; learning rate 5e-5; 3 epochs; batch size $1 \times$ gradient accumulation 8; warmup ratio 0.05; best checkpoint selected by validation loss.

\phantomsection\label{app:c3}\paragraph{C.3 Blinded-evaluation protocol, inter-judge agreement, and group-level tests (\Sref{sec:eval}).} Protocol: each sample is sent to each of the three judge APIs independently with a single prompt (May 2026; temperature $= 0.3$; GPT-5-nano does not support a temperature parameter and uses its default decoding). The prompt contains only the input and the text to be evaluated, without ground-truth source information, and asks the judge to infer the source and then provide two scores from 1 to 5 for sarcasm and human-likeness. Base and SFT outputs are evaluated independently with the same prompt. Main-model authorship F1 is 0.938 [0.931, 0.945] on the full set, 0.961 [0.952, 0.969] in Chinese, and 0.923 [0.912, 0.933] in English. Because the authorship label is constant within the human subset, positive-class F1 degenerates to the recall convention and is not separately reported. Chinese sarcasm F1 is substantially higher than English sarcasm F1 (0.819 vs.\ 0.429 for the main model) for two direct reasons. First, sarcasm prevalence is 63.8\% in the Chinese subset but only 14.2\% in the English subset. Positive-class F1 increases with prevalence, so the values for the two languages are not directly comparable. Second, the source benchmarks differ in difficulty: the English human side is dominated by intent-oriented iSarcasmEval tweets (the best systems on that benchmark achieve F1 of approximately 0.56--0.61; \Sref{sec:related-sarcasm}), whereas the Chinese side uses topic-comment-style ToSarcasm. Regarding the encoder, DeBERTa-v3's SentencePiece \citep{kudo2018sentencepiece} vocabulary tokenizes Chinese largely at the character level (measured at approximately 1.02 tokens per character, with approximately 2.5\% out-of-vocabulary symbols). The Chinese subset nevertheless achieves high F1 under these conditions, consistent with the dominant roles of prevalence and genre. The difference between overall and human-subset F1 (0.663 vs.\ 0.629 for the main model) comes from the contribution of AI-side instances; accordingly, the metric clarification in \Sref{sec:backbone} notes that the overall value aggregates both source sides. On the human subset, dual- versus single-head F1 differences are within 0.03 and vary in direction. The visible loss from negative transfer on the sarcasm side is concentrated in the AI-side quadrants, consistent with the class-conditional characterization in \Sref{sec:quadrant}.

False-positive rates use a threshold of 0.5. Instance-level score distributions show that the failure is not a threshold artifact: for the plain dual-head model on the AI $\times$ non-sarcastic subset, the median sarcasm probability is 0.767 (interquartile range 0.635--0.839), so the entire distribution shifts rightward across the threshold. The single-head medians are 0.204 (plain) / 0.069 (RCNN). At the same time, AUC values for ranking sarcasm probability within the AI side are similar across models (0.82--0.88): joint training mainly raises the level of sarcasm scores on AI texts while largely preserving their internal ordering, consistent with the characterization that a source-related component is added to the sarcasm signal.

\section{Preliminary Representation-Level Analysis of the Failure Mechanism}
\label{app:repr}

This appendix records the complete protocol, data, and self-checks for the representation-level tests in \Sref{sec:mechanism}. The interpretation of conclusions follows \Sref{sec:mechanism}.

\paragraph{E.1 Setup.} All analyses use the frozen model that produces the 86.3\% result for the plain dual-head model in the main text, without updating its parameters. Direction estimation and metric evaluation are cross-fitted (the direction is estimated on the fitting half and metrics are calculated on the evaluation half), so the baseline false-positive rate in this appendix (84.8\% on the evaluation half) differs slightly from the full-set value in the main text (86.3\%). Code self-checks: manually computed logits match model outputs elementwise (maximum error $1.4 \times 10^{-6}$); the erasure-projection residual is approximately $10^{-6}$; LEACE satisfies its defining property (source AUC on the fitting half: 0.988 $\to$ 0.042); and probe selectivity passes (true labels: 0.986; permuted labels: 0.512).

\paragraph{E.2 Protocol.} We record here the principles, citations, and implementation details for each test underlying \Sref{sec:mechanism}. The linear probe is logistic regression with five-fold cross-validation. The selectivity control retrains the same probe with permuted source labels. Correlation analyses are rechecked after stratification by text-length tertile. Alignment is the directional cosine under whitened geometry ($\Sigma^{1/2}w$). Erasure interventions include a single direction, the INLP subspace \citep[$k = 1$--$16$;][]{ravfogel2020inlp}, and LEACE \citep{belrose2023leace}. Placebos comprise ten random whitened directions and erasure after re-estimating directions from permuted labels. srcORTH-PC1 is the maximum-variance direction after orthogonalization against the source direction; erasing it does not alter source decodability and is used to distinguish the effects of ``removing the source concept'' and ``perturbing the principal-variance structure.''

\paragraph{E.3 Diagnostic evidence.} Source is highly linearly decodable: AUC 0.986 (sarcastic subset: 0.990 / non-sarcastic subset: 0.985; permuted-label control: 0.512; single-head model: 0.974, meaning that decodability is not unique to joint training). On AI $\times$ non-sarcastic instances, the correlation between source logit and sarcasm logit is $\rho = 0.519$ (0.44/0.56/0.63 after controlling by text-length tertile). The sarcasm decision direction and source direction are nearly orthogonal in the original geometry ($\cos \approx -0.03$) but moderately aligned in whitened geometry ($|\cos| = 0.41$ when measured using the probe direction / 0.49 using the authorship-head direction). All are compatible with the shortcut hypothesis, but all constitute correlational evidence.

\paragraph{E.4 Intervention evidence.} $\text{gap} =$ AI $\times$ non-sarcastic false-positive rate $-$ human $\times$ non-sarcastic false-positive rate; source AUC is the decoding ability of a probe retrained after erasure.

Interpretation: single-direction and low-dimensional INLP erasure are ineffective. LEACE initially appears effective (source is indeed erased, the gap falls from 52 to 19, and the placebos are all ineffective). However, erasing a high-variance direction orthogonal to source (leaving source information entirely intact) likewise reduces the false-positive rate to 66.7 and the gap to 12.6, an effect almost identical to that of LEACE.

\paragraph{E.5 Supplementary observation.} The mean false-positive rate across the two non-sarcastic source sides is almost unchanged before and after the intervention (58.8 $\to$ 59.1). The intervention does not ``fix'' the model; it merely transfers part of the overprediction of sarcasm on the AI side to the human side.

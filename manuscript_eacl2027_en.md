# When Joint Training with Machine-Generated Text Detection Can Distort Sarcasm Detection: A Bilingual Diagnosis of Class-Conditional Negative Transfer

## Abstract <!-- budget: ~0.35 pages -->

Sarcasm detection and machine-generated text detection (MGTD) are typically studied separately, yet LLM-era systems may need both judgments on the same text, and their joint learning under a shared encoder is unexamined. We build a bilingual Chinese–English dual-head detector and the diagnostic test set DualTest (4,664 instances), stratified into four `is_sarcastic × is_human` quadrants, to expose class-conditional failures that aggregate metrics obscure. Joint training induces a class-conditional negative transfer: the sarcasm head misclassifies 86.3% of AI-generated text prompted to be non-sarcastic as sarcastic, against 19.3% for a single-head model with the same sarcasm supervision but no joint training; the failure replicates on a second generator (91.3%) and is mitigated by two structurally distinct intermediate modules. Representation-level tests find source information highly decodable and correlated with sarcasm decisions, but concept-erasure effects are matched by erasing a high-variance direction orthogonal to source; the failure is better characterized as a source-correlated, style-entangled shortcut in a dominant-variance subspace, and the source concept's specific causal role remains a hypothesis. Detector scores also systematically disagree with three blinded LLM judges (the detector rates SFT outputs as more human-like, the judges the opposite; near-zero Chinese sarcasm correlation), so MGTD detectors should not directly serve as evaluation or reward signals for sarcasm generation.

## 1 Introduction <!-- budget: ~1 page -->

Sarcasm is a prototypical form of non-literal expression: speakers often convey an intended negative meaning through superficially positive wording. Because stable lexical cues are lacking, sarcasm detection has long depended on context, intent, and background knowledge, and annotation agreement is relatively low; multilingual settings amplify these difficulties further. The proliferation of LLMs introduces an additional source dimension: sarcastic social-media text may be written either by a human or generated by a model under instruction. In real-world deployment, “is this sarcastic?” and “was this written by a human?” are therefore no longer entirely independent questions, but judgments that may be required simultaneously of the same input text. The coupling also appears on the generation side: detector scores are already used in practice as evaluation and even reward signals for sarcasm generation (e.g., ViSP; Wang et al., 2026; §2.4), and judging whether generated sarcasm reads as human-written itself involves both dimensions at once. Yet existing work largely treats sentiment/rhetorical analysis, MGTD, and content-safety analysis as separate problems separately, and research that unifies sarcasm detection and MGTD as a joint multi-task learning problem remains absent (see §2.2 for the search protocol).

This paper investigates the risks of shared representations in the joint detection setting. When a single textual backbone serves both “source identification” and “semantic judgment,” the representation shaped by one task may alter the behavior of the other task on particular subgroups. This risk is general and not tied to a particular architecture. The dual-head model serves as a diagnostic vehicle that makes the risk observable and localizable with minimal structural complexity. In this setting, we diagnose a form of class-conditional negative transfer: after joint training with the MGTD task, the sarcasm head systematically misclassifies AI-generated non-sarcastic text as sarcastic. This error is induced by joint training, persists when the AI test texts are regenerated by a second generator, and can be mitigated by intermediate modules. At the mechanism level, probing and concept erasure show that source information is highly decodable from the shared representation, significantly correlated with sarcasm decisions, and aligned with the decision direction in whitened geometry, while intervention experiments locate the signal within a dominant-variance subspace, deeply entangled with stylistic factors (§5.3.2, Appendix E). We therefore position “the sarcasm head exploits a source-correlated shortcut” as a mechanism hypothesis with preliminary support; confirming it will require causal mediation and nonlinear tools and is left to dedicated study. An orthogonal control connects this analysis to methodological critiques of probing (Hewitt & Liang, 2019; Elazar et al., 2021) by demonstrating concretely what separates “decodable and decision-correlated” from an established mechanism.

**Contributions.** Following the structure of the paper, our contributions are fourfold:

- **A bilingual dataset suite and dual-head diagnostic setting for joint detection (§3–§4).** Four bilingual datasets: SarcTrain (10,000 sarcasm-labeled human texts), AuthTrain (19,995; 9,996 retained AI counterparts for 9,999 human seeds), GenTrain (2,428 generation instructions), and the diagnostic test set DualTest (4,664), whose instances carry both `is_sarcastic` and `is_human` labels and support class-conditional analysis over the four “source × sarcasm” quadrants. To the best of our knowledge, DualTest is the first dual-label stratified test set for sarcasm evaluation, and the dual-head model built on it is the first to formulate the two tasks as a joint multi-task problem (§2.2).
- **Behavioral diagnosis of class-conditional negative transfer (§5).** The plain dual-head model misclassifies 86.3% of AI-generated non-sarcastic texts as sarcastic; a single-head model with the same sarcasm supervision misclassifies only 19.3%. With replication on a second generator and mitigation by two structurally distinct intermediate modules, these results establish that the failure is induced by joint training, and the three-architecture comparison tests the intermediate-layer buffering hypothesis.
- **Representation-level characterization of the failure and boundaries on mechanism attribution (§5.3.2).** Source information is highly decodable from the sarcasm representation and significantly correlated with sarcasm decisions, yet concept erasure and an orthogonal high-variance control show that the intervention effect is not specific to the source concept, characterizing the failure as a source-correlated, style-entangled shortcut and revealing the gap between “decodable and correlated” and “mechanistically attributable.”
- **An empirical warning about MGTD detectors as generation-evaluation signals (§6).** Detector scores systematically disagree with three blinded LLM judges: the detector rates SFT outputs as more human-like, the judges the opposite, and the Chinese sarcasm correlation is near zero, an empirical warning against using detector scores as generation rewards (§2.4).

## 2 Related Work <!-- budget: ~0.95 pages -->

### 2.1 Sarcasm Detection and Multilingual Resources

Feature-engineered methods (Riloff et al., 2013), neural sequence models (Ghosh & Veale, 2016; Tay et al., 2018), and pretrained language models (Khatri & P, 2020; Han et al., 2022) have each advanced sarcasm detection. The task remains difficult in multilingual and class-imbalanced settings, especially when labels target intended rather than perceived sarcasm. Although scores are not directly comparable across datasets, languages, modalities, or evaluation setups, they illustrate substantial benchmark variability: the top two English systems on iSarcasmEval (Abu Farha et al., 2022) report positive-class F1 of 0.605 and 0.569; zero-shot ChatGPT positive-class F1 ranges from 0.152 to 0.567 across MultiPICo languages (Casola et al., 2024); and a text-only XLM-R baseline reaches 0.734 F1 on the bilingual multimodal SarcNet benchmark (Yao et al., 2024). Yang and Ikeda (2024), building on their earlier multilingual mBERT/BiLSTM study (Yang and Ikeda, 2023), compared mBERT-RCNN (mBERT with the recurrent convolutional architecture of Lai et al., 2015) on mixed English–Arabic and English–Chinese training sets and reported improved sarcasm-detection performance on two English test sets. We therefore include RCNN among the intermediate architectures examined in §4.1. Because these benchmark results come from different datasets, §5.1 provides directly comparable mBERT dual-head baselines on DualTest. We use a bilingual Chinese–English setting to test whether the subgroup failure is confined to one language, rather than treating cross-language transfer as the primary objective.

### 2.2 Machine-Generated Text Detection and Authorship Verification

Machine-generated text detection (MGTD) uses zero-shot/perplexity-based detectors (Mitchell et al., 2023) and discriminative models trained on paired human–AI corpora (HC3; Guo et al., 2023); generalization is the recognized central challenge, motivating M4 (Wang et al., 2024) for black-box detection across generators, domains, and languages and RAID (Dugan et al., 2024) for robust detector evaluation. Conceptually, Bevendorff et al. (2025) frame the problem as authorship verification rather than quality assessment; we treat MGTD as an auxiliary task within a shared encoder. Prior work treats sarcasm detection and MGTD as unrelated problems: existing multi-task sarcasm research mainly combines sarcasm with sentiment, stance, or emotion, and to the best of our knowledge no work has studied the two as a joint multi-task problem.[^search] How a shared encoder behaves when both judgments are learned jointly is therefore an open question and the starting point of this paper.

[^search]: The search was conducted in May 2026 across Semantic Scholar, arXiv, ACL Anthology, and Google Scholar, using keyword combinations including “sarcasm detection,” “AI-generated text detection,” “authorship detection,” “multi-task learning,” and “joint detection.”

### 2.3 Multi-Task Negative Transfer, Shortcut Learning, and Shared-Encoder Mitigation

We use negative transfer and shortcut learning as explanatory frameworks for a previously unstudied task combination. In multi-task learning, auxiliary tasks are not always beneficial: transfer direction depends on task relatedness and data geometry (Zhang et al., 2023; Wu, Zhang, & Ré, 2020), and conflicting gradients can cause negative transfer (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021). Mueller et al. (2022) provide the closest NLP precedent, showing through single-/multi-task contrasts that models may infer task identity from the input distribution and destabilize on out-of-distribution inputs (see also He & Choi, 2021; Li et al., 2023, on internal mechanisms of multi-task models). Shortcut learning is complementary: models may exploit features predictive in the training distribution but failing on specific subgroups (Geirhos et al., 2020; Ye et al., 2024), which in multi-task settings appears as increased absorption of spurious correlations (Hu et al., 2022b; Chai & Wang, 2025) and subgroup-stratified failures (Compton et al., 2023). Shared-encoder mitigation forms a continuum from task-specific parameter isolation (AdapterFusion, Pfeiffer et al., 2021; MAD-X, Pfeiffer et al., 2020; HyperFormer++, Karimi Mahabadi et al., 2021; PALs, Stickland & Murray, 2019) to gating, attention, or intermediate modules that filter interference in shared representations (Wu et al., 2019; CA-MTL, Pilault et al., 2021); our intermediate-layer analysis sits nearer the latter end. We connect these lines: conditional stratification exposes a subgroup-specific failure induced by joint training, and controlled comparisons of three intermediate structures test the mitigation hypothesis (§5.3).

### 2.4 Misalignment in Detector-Based Evaluation and Rewards

Beyond the main line of failure diagnosis, dual-head detector outputs might also be used as generation-evaluation or reward signals; this section gives the background for the supplementary analysis in §6. LLM-as-a-judge (Zheng et al., 2023) is widely used for generation evaluation, and using automated scorers as rewards risks reward hacking in the Goodhart sense. The practice has entered sarcasm generation: ViSP (Wang et al., 2026) uses scores from the sarcasm detector DIP (Wen et al., 2023) as a PPO reward, its data filtering and parts of its quality analysis rely on the same detector, and its authors list “performance depends on the quality of the external evaluator” as their primary limitation. Since MGTD targets verification rather than quality assessment (§2.2), agreement between detector scores and perceived quality requires a direct test; §6 provides it and analyzes the systematic directional bias that appears when an MGTD detector is used as a generation-evaluation signal.

## 3 Dataset Construction <!-- budget: ~0.9 pages -->

We begin with five heterogeneous sources (iSarcasm: Oprea & Magdy, 2020 / Abu Farha et al., 2022; CSC: Jang & Frassinelli, 2024 / Jang et al., 2023; ToSarcasm: Liang et al., 2022; News Headlines v2: Misra & Arora, 2023 / Misra & Grover, 2021; Open Chinese Internet Sarcasm Corpus: Zhu, 2022; 44,862 instances after standardized merging, approximately 87% English) and obtain three training sets and one diagnostic test set through a three-stage pipeline. Source-level details, annotation methods, and quality checks are provided in Appendix A.

**Sarcasm classification training set, SarcTrain (10,000, all human).** The dataset is balanced across four language × label buckets, with 2,500 instances per bucket, and is pre-split into five folds. Three buckets are downsampled; the Chinese sarcastic bucket is oversampled from 2,394 unique instances.

**MGTD training set, AuthTrain (19,995).** The human side is sampled directly from SarcTrain with language balance. On the AI side, deepseek-r1:8b (DeepSeek-AI, 2025, via Ollama) is instructed to produce a topically related output from each human seed: 9,996 outputs are retained for 9,999 human instances (99.97% pairing coverage), while three generation failures remain only on the human side. Four templates (Chinese/English × sarcastic/normal) follow the balanced seed labels. Authorship and requested-style labels are constructed as separate, approximately balanced factors; no independence is assumed for realized textual features or perceived sarcasm. Generation parameters, full templates, and post-processing steps are given in Appendix A.

**Generation training set, GenTrain (2,428; used in §6).** This set is drawn from two sources containing topic or contextual information (iSarcasm and ToSarcasm; see Appendix A for their uses). Only positive sarcasm instances are retained; approximately 40% are sampled, and the minority language is oversampled to 1,214 instances in each of Chinese and English. By prompt type, the set contains 374 rewriting, 839 response, and 1,215 comment instances, and serves as the instruction-tuning data for SFT.

**Diagnostic test set, DualTest (4,664).** This is the core data contribution of the paper: each instance bears both `is_sarcastic` and `is_human` labels. The human side comprises the official iSarcasm test split (1,400 English instances) and the ToSarcasm test set (972 Chinese instances). Generation and quality control retain deepseek-r1:8b counterparts for 2,292 of the 2,372 human texts (96.6% coverage). Human texts retain their annotations; for AI texts, `is_sarcastic` records the prompted condition rather than an independently annotated judgment of perceived sarcasm, and `is_human` is false by construction. After combining languages, the four quadrants contain 823 human × sarcastic, 1,549 human × non-sarcastic, 779 AI × sarcastic, and 1,513 AI × non-sarcastic instances (see Appendix A for the language × source × sarcasm distribution).

## 4 Dual-Head Detection Model <!-- budget: ~0.9 pages -->

### 4.1 Tasks and Architecture

Given a Chinese or English input text $x$, the model produces two separate, non-mutually-exclusive binary predictions: whether $x$ is labeled sarcastic and whether its authorship source is labeled human or AI. Any combination of the two labels is valid. The tasks share a single DeBERTa-v3-large encoder (He et al., 2023; this encoder was pretrained on English corpora, and §5.1 directly compares it with multilingual-pretrained mBERT / XLM-R on the same bilingual test set). Encoder outputs are aggregated by an intermediate module and then passed to two task-specific classification heads; Figure 1 shows the main model.

![Figure 1](figures/fig_architecture.png)

**Figure 1: Main dual-head model: a shared DeBERTa-v3-large encoder, an RCNN intermediate module (dashed), and two task-specific heads. The comparisons in §5 remove this component in the plain configuration or replace it with task-branch interaction projection, while retaining the same encoder backbone and task definitions (Appendix B).**

The main model uses BiLSTM + CNN (RCNN) as its intermediate module. A single-layer bidirectional LSTM with hidden size 256 per direction produces a 512-dimensional output at each token position. We concatenate it with the 1,024-dimensional encoder output, then apply three groups of one-dimensional convolutions with kernel widths 3/4/5 and 128 channels each. Global max pooling produces a 384-dimensional vector $z$; after dropout, $z$ is passed to the two heads. This module adapts the mBERT-RCNN intermediate architecture of Yang and Ikeda (2024) to a DeBERTa-based dual-head setting. The plain variant sends `[CLS]` directly to both heads. The task-branch interaction projection variant forms two learned 256-dimensional projections, $h_s$ and $h_a$, and updates them as $h_s'=h_s+P_{a\rightarrow s}(h_a)$ and $h_a'=h_a+P_{s\rightarrow a}(h_s)$. Each branch contains one vector, so no query–key interaction remains; $P$ is the effective learned value/output projection. The configurations retain the same encoder backbone and task definitions, but their intermediate representations, head input dimensions, and parameter counts differ; remaining dimensions and dropout settings are in Appendix B.

### 4.2 Training Protocol

Training uses a mixed loader that alternates batches by task: each batch contains samples from only one task, and MGTD and sarcasm batches alternate in a 2:1 ratio, matching the data-size ratio between AuthTrain and SarcTrain. Let the shared parameters be $\theta_{\mathrm{sh}}$ (the encoder and intermediate module), let $z(x)$ be their aggregated representation for input $x$, and let $W_s$ and $W_a$ denote the two classification heads. At step $t$, the loss is computed only for the head corresponding to the task of the current batch $B_t$:

$$
\mathcal{L}(B_t)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{|B_t|}\displaystyle\sum_{(x,y)\in B_t}\mathrm{CE}_{w}\bigl(\mathrm{softmax}(W_s\,z(x)),\,y\bigr), & B_t\subset \text{SarcTrain},\\[8pt]
\dfrac{1}{|B_t|}\displaystyle\sum_{(x,y)\in B_t}\mathrm{CE}\bigl(\mathrm{softmax}(W_a\,z(x)),\,y\bigr), & B_t\subset \text{AuthTrain},
\end{cases}
$$

where $\mathrm{CE}_w$ is class-weighted cross-entropy that compensates for the scarcity of sarcastic positives (weights are provided in Appendix B). The gradient flow follows directly from the equation: on sarcasm batches, $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial W_a = 0$ (symmetrically, $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial W_s = 0$ on MGTD batches), whereas $\theta_{\mathrm{sh}}$ receives gradients from both types of batches. The shared encoder and intermediate module are shaped jointly by both tasks, while each classification head is updated only by data from its own task.

Optimization uses separate learning rates for parameter groups: the pretrained encoder is updated at a much lower rate than the intermediate module and classification heads to preserve pretrained representations. Model selection accounts for both tasks. The early-stopping signal at epoch $e$ is the equally weighted sum of each task's validation loss normalized by its first-epoch value:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{stop}}^{(e)}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{\mathcal{L}_{s}^{(e)}}{\mathcal{L}_{s}^{(1)}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{\mathcal{L}_{a}^{(e)}}{\mathcal{L}_{a}^{(1)}},
$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{s}^{(e)}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{a}^{(e)}$ are the epoch-$e$ validation losses for the sarcasm and MGTD tasks on their respective validation sets. Normalization prevents early stopping from being dominated by the larger MGTD task.

Training and evaluation follow a “five independent runs with a fixed external evaluation set” protocol. SarcTrain is pre-split into five folds: run $k$ trains the sarcasm task on four folds, with the held-out fold as the sarcasm validation set providing $\mathcal{L}_{s}^{(e)}$; AuthTrain uses a fixed 90% training / 10% validation split shared by all runs, its validation portion providing $\mathcal{L}_{a}^{(e)}$, so between-run variation is concentrated in changes to the sarcasm training data. Each run is trained from a fresh initialization of the same pretrained base; all architectures and ablations share the fold assignments and seed scheme, and cross-model comparisons are paired by split. The five models are then evaluated on both tasks on the external test set DualTest, which is involved in no split. The main tables report the mean ± standard deviation across the five runs (hereafter, five-run mean ± standard deviation); this standard deviation reflects between-run variation from training splits and initialization, while test-set sampling uncertainty is quantified separately through confidence intervals for the final model (Table 3 and Appendix C.7).

A separate final model, used only for subsequent inference and rescoring (§5.3 quadrant diagnosis and §6 scoring), is trained on all of SarcTrain and all of AuthTrain with the same hyperparameters and a fixed number of epochs (the mode of the five runs' best early-stopping epochs + 1), without any selection based on DualTest; its results do not enter the main tables. Class weights, sampling ratio, grouped learning rates, and full hyperparameters are provided in Appendix B.

## 5 Experiments and Diagnosis <!-- budget: ~2.9 pages -->

We first compare encoder backbones, joint versus single-task training, and intermediate-layer architectural variants using overall F1. We then use DualTest's dual labels to partition the same evaluation into four “source × sarcasm” quadrants, diagnosing a class-conditional failure that aggregate metrics cannot reveal. Overall metrics below are five-run means ± standard deviations; quadrant metrics are point estimates from the final model.

### 5.1 Backbone Comparison

We compare dual-head models across four encoder backbones (mBERT, XLM-RoBERTa-base, and DeBERTa-v3 base and large) and, on the large encoder, the two intermediate-module variants of §4.1. The encoders span multilingual and English-centric pretraining: mBERT is BERT-base pretrained on multilingual Wikipedia (Devlin et al., 2019), XLM-RoBERTa-base is pretrained on CommonCrawl text covering about one hundred languages (Conneau et al., 2020), and DeBERTa-v3 combines disentangled attention with ELECTRA-style pretraining on primarily English corpora. “Sarcasm F1” is the F1 of the sarcastic class (positive class), following the official iSarcasmEval convention, and “MGTD F1” is the F1 of the human class (positive class); both are computed over the full DualTest set, aggregating Chinese and English and both source sides, with AI-side sarcasm labels constructed from the generation instructions (§3).

Under the parameter scales and hyperparameter settings used here, DeBERTa-family encoders clearly outperform mBERT and XLM-R; the comparison mixes differences in parameter scale and pretraining corpora, and serves here to select the diagnostic vehicle. The two intermediate-layer variants differ little from the plain dual-head model in their means (with the main RCNN model performing better), but reduce between-run variance by approximately 34–47%. Chinese sarcasm F1 is substantially higher than English sarcasm F1 (0.819 vs. 0.429 for the main model), primarily because of differences in sarcasm prevalence between the two language subsets (63.8% vs. 14.2%) and differences in source-benchmark difficulty (Appendix C.7). ### 5.3 Four-Quadrant Diagnosis: Class-Conditional Negative Transfer and Intermediate-Layer Buffering

Aggregate metrics obscure the structure of errors. The overall sarcasm F1 of the plain dual-head model alone would merely suggest mediocre performance, although its errors are in fact concentrated on the AI-text subset. To localize this class-conditional failure, we partition DualTest by the ground-truth `is_human` × `is_sarcastic` labels into four quadrant subsets and examine the detector's sarcasm predictions separately within each. Because each quadrant contains only one ground-truth class, F1 degenerates on such subsets (precision is always 1 on positive-class subsets, while negative-class subsets contain no positive instances). We therefore report recall for positive-class subsets and the false-positive rate for negative-class subsets.

The single-task control and cross-generator replication establish the behavioral conclusion: class-conditional negative transfer exists and is induced by joint training. Representation-level tests provide three pieces of correlational evidence for the explanatory hypothesis that “the sarcasm head exploits a source-correlated shortcut,” and locate the signal as deeply entangled with the dominant-variance structure of the representation.

#### 5.3.1 Three Architectures and Single-Head Controls

We compare the sarcasm predictions of five detectors on the same DualTest set. The three dual-head variants (plain / interaction-projection / RCNN) characterize how the severity of the failure changes with intermediate structure. The two single-head models share the same architecture and sarcasm supervision data (SarcTrain) as their corresponding dual-head models. They differ only in whether the auxiliary MGTD task is jointly trained (that task's training objective and the AuthTrain data are added as a whole). The single-head model addresses a counterfactual question: how would the same sarcasm classifier behave without joint training? If the failure stemmed from the sarcasm training data (SarcTrain contains only human text), the single-head model should fail in the same way. If the failure is induced by joint training, the single-head model should be unaffected. Table 3 reports sarcasm predictions. The failure is concentrated in the sarcasm head; the MGTD head remains stable under the same partition, serving as a control for the failure's directionality, as reported below and in Appendix C.

**Table 3: Comparison of five sarcasm classifiers on the four ground-truth quadrant subsets (recall for sarcastic subsets; false-positive rate for non-sarcastic subsets)**

| Model (sarcasm prediction) | Human × sarcastic recall | Human × non-sarcastic false positive | AI × sarcastic recall | **AI × non-sarcastic false positive** |
|------------|----------------|------------------|--------------|--------------------|
| Plain dual-head | 67.1% | 32.9% | 98.2% | **86.3%** [84.5, 88.0] |
| Interaction-projection dual-head | 71.9% | 37.6% | 94.4% | **40.9%** [38.5, 43.4] |
| RCNN dual-head (main) | 80.8% | 40.5% | 89.3% | **34.1%** [31.8, 36.5] |
| Plain single-head (sarcasm only) | 67.8% | 28.7% | 76.6% | **19.3%** [17.4, 21.4] |
| RCNN single-head (sarcasm only) | 66.2% | 28.5% | 79.1% | **19.3%** [17.4, 21.4] |

Brackets in the final column give Wilson 95% confidence intervals over test instances. Intervals for all quadrants, paired tests on the same texts, and score distributions for each quadrant (showing that the failure is an overall rightward shift of the score distribution rather than a threshold artifact) are provided in Appendix C.7.

The plain dual-head model fails severely in the AI × non-sarcastic quadrant: it misclassifies 86.3% of the instances as sarcastic, indicating that on this subset the model likely relies on cues associated with AI source rather than stable sarcastic semantics. Both structurally distinct intermediate modules substantially mitigate the failure, reducing it by more than 45 pp from 86.3%, with mutually non-overlapping confidence intervals across the rates (Table 3). The approximately 7 pp gap between RCNN and interaction projection is smaller but remains significant (Appendix C.7). The single-head models answer the counterfactual question above: despite also being trained only on human text, the single-head models have a false-positive rate of just 19.3%. The difference from the plain dual-head model on the same texts is significant by a paired McNemar test (discordant pairs 1,021 / 7, $p < 0.001$; exact $p$ values and the remaining pairs are reported in Appendix C.7). This rules out “the sarcasm training data lack AI text” as an explanation and shows that the failure is induced by joint training. Moreover, the RCNN and plain single-head models behave almost identically (both approximately 19.3%), indicating that RCNN's mitigating effect emerges in the presence of joint training rather than from inherent architectural robustness to AI text.

The MGTD head provides a control for the failure's directionality. On the same AI × non-sarcastic subset, its false-positive rate is only 6.1% (plain) / 6.6% (RCNN), and it remains stable across all four quadrants; the single-head MGTD counterfactual performs at the same level as the dual-head models (complete values in Appendix C). On the same set of AI texts, the problem is concentrated in the sarcasm head and the gap is on the order of tens of percentage points. The failure is jointly conditioned on source and class and is most extreme on the AI × non-sarcastic subset, hence the term “class-conditional negative transfer.” This subset-level failure and the asymmetric aggregate transfer in §5.2 are the same negative transfer seen at two scales.

#### 5.3.2 Mechanism Explanation and Levels of Evidence

Based on the behavioral evidence in §5.3.1, we propose a mechanism hypothesis. Under the gradient flow of §4.2, the shared encoder and intermediate module continuously receive MGTD gradients pushing them toward AI-versus-human style discrimination, while the sarcasm head, updated only on the all-human SarcTrain, never receives a gradient on AI text; reading a representation shaped by the MGTD task, it may misuse source-collinear cues, not intrinsically related to sarcasm, as sarcasm signals. This is the failure form characterized in the shortcut-learning literature (§2.3), except that here the shortcut is introduced by joint training and triggered conditionally (AI source × non-sarcastic). Under this hypothesis the intermediate layer buffers part of the backbone perturbation; a single-head model has no perturbation to buffer.

We test the hypothesis on the frozen plain dual-head model; Appendix E records the full protocol, per-test tables, and self-checks. Three diagnostic tests place source information inside the decision. A linear probe on the representation read by the sarcasm head decodes text source at AUC 0.986, versus 0.512 with permuted labels (a selectivity control; Hewitt & Liang, 2019) and 0.974 on the single-head model, so genuine source information is present, though decodability itself is not unique to joint training. The projection of the representation onto the source direction correlates with the sarcasm logit on the AI × non-sarcastic subset (Spearman 0.52, robust within text-length tertiles, excluding a length confound). And in whitened geometry, where a linear head's decision reduces to a direction comparison, the source and sarcasm decision directions align at $|\cos| \approx$ 0.41–0.49. Erasure interventions then ask whether removing source information removes the failure. Single directions and 16-dimensional INLP (iterative removal of source-predictive directions; Ravfogel et al., 2020) leave the false-positive rate unreduced; LEACE (a minimal linear transformation making the concept linearly undecodable; Belrose et al., 2023) cuts source decodability from AUC 0.982 to 0.589 and the false-positive rate from 84.8% to 68.7%, while random-direction and permuted-label placebos have no effect. Considered alone, this supports a causal source account, but the srcORTH-PC1 orthogonal control gives contrary evidence: erasing a high-variance direction orthogonal to source leaves source decodability intact (AUC 0.982) yet likewise reduces the false-positive rate to 66.7%, almost matching LEACE. The common ingredient of the effective interventions is perturbation of the dominant-variance subspace, not removal of the source concept.

The representation-level tests therefore partially support and simultaneously constrain the source-shortcut hypothesis: the failure is better characterized as a shortcut located in a dominant-variance subspace, collinear with source, and entangled with stylistic factors than as a simple causal dependence on source identity; source-specific attribution will require causal mediation and nonlinear tools and is left to dedicated study (throughout the paper we call this component “source-correlated”). Consistent with critiques based on selectivity controls (Hewitt & Liang, 2019) and amnesic probing (Elazar et al., 2021), the orthogonal control demonstrates concretely that “decodable, correlated, and responsive to intervention” does not by itself establish a mechanism, and orthogonal controls should become standard in concept-erasure analyses. Irrespective of the preferred mechanism interpretation, the differences in false-positive rates among the three architectures in this quadrant remain observational facts.

#### 5.3.3 Cross-Generator Replication

To test whether the failure depends on a particular generator, we regenerate the AI side of DualTest with another generator, qwen3.5:9b (keeping the human side unchanged), and recompute the four quadrants with the same trained detectors; Figure 2 plots the five detectors' false-positive rates on both generators with Wilson 95% confidence intervals, and Appendix C.7 tabulates them with paired tests. The core failure increases rather than decreases with the new generator (86.3% → 91.3%), while the single-head false-positive rate on the same texts falls (19.3% → 7.9%), widening the dual-/single-head gap from about 67 pp to about 83 pp. Because the single-head model classifies the same AI texts more accurately, the 91.3% value is more consistent with joint training having altered the sarcasm head's behavior than with text difficulty or superficial fingerprints. The direction and relative ordering of mitigation by intermediate modules are preserved (RCNN: 34.1% → 22.1%; interaction projection: 40.9% → 29.5%). The failure thus does not depend on a single generator.

## 6 Generation Evaluation and Detector–Judge Misalignment <!-- budget: ~0.4 pages -->

This section discusses a question related to but independent of §5. Section 5 diagnosed the detector's class-conditional failure as a classifier; here we test whether the same detector, when used as a generation-evaluation signal, aligns with an independent quality evaluation provided by three blinded LLM judges (proxies for human judgment; see Limitations). The failure diagnosed in §5 is one identified source of any misalignment, but not the only one. This test has direct practical relevance: detector scores have already been used as generation rewards (e.g., ViSP; Wang et al., 2026; §2.4), and the continuous sarcasm and human-likeness scores output by our dual-head model could in principle serve as such multidimensional evaluation signals; the premise under test is whether they can represent generation quality.

We use Qwen3-8B (Qwen Team, 2025) as the base model and fine-tune it on GenTrain using QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023). On an independent set of 300 prompts (180 Chinese topic-comment generation prompts and 120 English normal-sentence rewriting prompts; composition in Appendix A), outputs are scored by the main-model detector and blindly evaluated by three LLMs (DeepSeek-V3, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, and GPT-5-nano) on the same samples (sarcasm and human-likeness each scored from 1 to 5; judge protocol and inter-judge agreement in Appendix C.3). SFT configurations are provided in Appendix B, and complete detector scores and the analysis of language asymmetry are in Appendix C.

The two types of scores exhibit direct ranking contradictions. In Chinese, the detector's sarcasm score increases after SFT (0.780 → 0.895), while the blinded mean score from the three LLMs decreases (4.01 → 3.71). “SFT sarcasm is rated below the base model” is significant in five of the six groups (2 languages × 3 evaluators) under Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995; adjusted $p < 0.001$; group-level tests in Appendix C), indicating a directional reversal rather than numerical noise. On the MGTD dimension, detector scores rise sharply after SFT in both languages (the detector judges SFT outputs as “more human-like”), again contradicting the direction of the blinded judgments. Table 4 quantifies this misalignment using Spearman rank correlation (detector score vs. the mean of the three evaluators, $n = 600$, pooling the base and SFT conditions). Correlation is near zero on the Chinese sarcasm dimension and significantly negative on the human-likeness dimension in both languages. Recomputing correlations separately within the base and SFT conditions (Appendix C.5) separates the components: the negative MGTD correlation is driven mainly by opposite between-condition changes (SFT sharply raises detector scores while lowering blinded judgments); within the base condition the correlation is near zero, and within SFT it is weakly negative. Even within a single generation condition, then, detector scores show no positive alignment with judged human-likeness, and the clearest evidence of misalignment remains the condition-level ranking contradiction above. Correlation on the Chinese sarcasm dimension is at most weakly positive within conditions ($\leq +0.15$). The plain detector changes $\rho$ by at most 0.02 on every subset (Appendix C), so the misalignment is robust to detector architecture. Chinese sarcasm correlation remains near zero even after RCNN reduces the false-positive rate from 86% to 34%, indicating that the class-conditional failure is one source of misalignment rather than its sole mechanism. This comparison spans two evaluation settings (§5.3 on DualTest and this section on SFT-generated text); causal separation on the same samples is left for future work.

The misalignment originates in a mismatch between training distributions and measurement dimensions. What the detector learns as “human-like” is proximity to the token distribution of the human training corpus, and SFT outputs imitate precisely this distribution, hence are classified as human. In contrast, the LLM judges' notion of “human-like” is based on semantic coherence and naturalness. The two systems measure different dimensions of “human-likeness” (representative examples in Appendix D, including a clearly AI-generated text with leaked chain-of-thought that the MGTD head still classifies as human with the highest confidence). A classifier learns a decision boundary, whereas generation evaluation requires naturalness and ironic intent; the two objectives are not equivalent. Using detector scores as generation rewards would incentivize the generator to approach the decision boundary rather than improve perceived quality, creating a risk of reward hacking in the Goodhart sense. Evaluation should therefore move toward integrating detector signals, LLM judgments, and human evaluation. For generation frameworks that use detector scores as rewards (such as ViSP), the direct implication is that alignment between the reward and independent quality judgments must be tested separately by language and dimension. The near-zero correlation on the Chinese sarcasm dimension means that a detector-reported “sarcasm improvement” may be disconnected from evaluator perception; independent evaluation makes such a disconnection visible.

## 7 Conclusion <!-- budget: ~0.45 pages -->

This paper studies joint modeling of sarcasm detection and MGTD and identifies and diagnoses a form of class-conditional negative transfer: after joint training with the MGTD task, the sarcasm head systematically misclassifies AI-generated non-sarcastic text as sarcastic. Single-head controls, cross-generator replication, and controlled comparisons across three architectures jointly localize this error to joint training itself. At the mechanism level, representation-level tests partially support and constrain the source-shortcut hypothesis: source information is highly decodable and aligned with the decision direction, but intervention effects are not specific to the source concept. The failure is therefore characterized as a source-correlated shortcut (§5.3.2). Supporting this diagnosis are the first dual-head joint architecture for sarcasm × MGTD and the first dual-label, four-quadrant diagnostic test set, DualTest. Additional observations include language asymmetry after SFT and systematic disagreement between detector scores and blinded LLM judgments; the latter provides an empirical warning against using MGTD detectors as generation-evaluation or reward signals. Future work proceeds along three directions: completing mechanism attribution using causal mediation and nonlinear analyses; specifically repairing the failure through parameter-isolating intermediate structures and data-side remedies (adding labeled AI text to sarcasm training); and incorporating the observed misalignment into reward design and testing its dynamic consequences through RL experiments.

## Limitations <!-- mandatory; excluded from page limit; must not contain new results -->

The first set of limitations concerns data and experimental settings. The AI-side sarcasm labels in DualTest are instruction-conditioned rather than independently annotated by humans, so their absolute scores should be read as performance relative to the requested generation style. The AI texts for the main results are produced by a single open-source generator (deepseek-r1:8b), while cross-generator replication (§5.3.3) extends the conclusion to a second local model. Numerical behavior and the absolute performance of the MGTD head on larger-scale or closed-source generators remain to be investigated across a broader generator family. In addition, the five data sources use somewhat inconsistent operational definitions of sarcasm (self-labeled tweets, distantly supervised news headlines, and third-party-annotated social-media text). The model input is a single text; introducing conversational context may change both performance and failure patterns.

The second set concerns the depth of the mechanism interpretation. Our argument is primarily behavioral. The mechanism concepts are inherited from the negative-transfer and shortcut-learning literature cited in §2.3, while our contribution is precise quantification in a new task combination and controlled comparisons across three architectures. Representation-level tests (Appendix E) characterize the failure as a shortcut entangled with stylistic factors and located in a dominant-variance subspace. Specifically attributing the failure to the source concept will require causal mediation and nonlinear analyses; establishing “buffering” as the mechanism of the intermediate layer will require a capacity-matched null intermediate-layer control and direct gradient analysis, and other intermediate structures such as hypernetworks and mixtures of experts were not tested. In addition, the single-head control treats the source task's training objective and AuthTrain data as a whole; decomposing their individual contributions (discriminative objective vs. exposure to the AI-text distribution) is left to future work.

The third set concerns the scale and proxy nature of generation evaluation. Section 6 evaluates 300 prompts using three blinded LLM judges. LLM judgments are proxies for, not replacements for, human judgments. The imbalance in Chinese and English SFT sample sizes (60:40) may partly explain the language asymmetry in detector scores (Appendix C.6). The direction of the misalignment warning is supported by consistency across multiple judges, while its quantitative precision awaits confirmation through larger-scale human evaluation.

```
【任务】生成一条讽刺风格的评论

- 表达与主题相关的观点即可，不必追求严谨
- 20-80字为佳
【评论】
```

```
[Task] Generate a sarcastic-style comment
[Topic] {seed}
[Requirements]
- Directly provide a short comment; do not explain your reasoning or analysis
- The style should be sarcastic, humorous, and exaggerated
- It is sufficient to express a viewpoint related to the topic; strict rigor is not required
- Preferably 20–80 Chinese characters
[Comment]
```

The Chinese non-sarcastic version changes only the style line to “The style should be normal and natural.” The two English templates parallel the Chinese templates (the style lines are “sarcastic, humorous, exaggerated” and “normal, natural,” respectively; length constraint: 10–50 words). Failed generations are discarded. AuthTrain contains three generation failures (9,996 complete pairs; the corresponding human instances are retained). ## Appendix E: Preliminary Representation-Level Analysis of the Failure Mechanism <!-- excluded from page limit; two columns -->

This appendix records the complete protocol, data, and self-checks for the representation-level tests in §5.3.2. The interpretation of conclusions follows §5.3.2.

**E.1 Setup.** All analyses use the frozen model that produces the 86.3% result for the plain dual-head model in the main text, without updating its parameters. Direction estimation and metric evaluation are cross-fitted (the direction is estimated on the fitting half and metrics are calculated on the evaluation half), so the baseline false-positive rate in this appendix (84.8% on the evaluation half) differs slightly from the full-set value in the main text (86.3%). Code self-checks: manually computed logits match model outputs elementwise (maximum error $1.4 \times 10^{-6}$); the erasure-projection residual is approximately $10^{-6}$; LEACE satisfies its defining property (source AUC on the fitting half: 0.988 → 0.042); and probe selectivity passes (true labels: 0.986; permuted labels: 0.512).

**E.2 Protocol.** Section 5.3.2 gives the principles and citations for each test; implementation details are recorded here. The linear probe is logistic regression with five-fold cross-validation. The selectivity control retrains the same probe with permuted source labels. Correlation analyses are rechecked after stratification by text-length tertile. Alignment is the directional cosine under whitened geometry ($\Sigma^{1/2}w$). Erasure interventions include a single direction, the INLP subspace ($k = 1$–$16$), and LEACE. Placebos comprise ten random whitened directions and erasure after re-estimating directions from permuted labels. srcORTH-PC1 is the maximum-variance direction after orthogonalization against the source direction; erasing it does not alter source decodability and is used to distinguish the effects of “removing the source concept” and “perturbing the principal-variance structure.”

**E.3 Diagnostic evidence.** Source is highly linearly decodable: AUC 0.986 (sarcastic subset: 0.990 / non-sarcastic subset: 0.985; permuted-label control: 0.512; single-head model: 0.974, meaning that decodability is not unique to joint training). On AI × non-sarcastic instances, the correlation between source logit and sarcasm logit is $\rho = 0.519$ (0.44/0.56/0.63 after controlling by text-length tertile). The sarcasm decision direction and source direction are nearly orthogonal in the original geometry ($\cos \approx -0.03$) but moderately aligned in whitened geometry ($|\cos| = 0.41$ when measured using the probe direction / 0.49 using the MGTD-head direction). All are compatible with the shortcut hypothesis, but all constitute correlational evidence.

**E.4 Intervention evidence.** $\text{gap} =$ AI × non-sarcastic false-positive rate − human × non-sarcastic false-positive rate; source AUC is the decoding ability of a probe retrained after erasure.

| Erasure scheme | AI × non-sarcastic false positive | Human × non-sarcastic false positive | Gap | Source AUC (after) |
|----------|----------------|------------------|------|----------------|
| None (baseline) | 84.8 | 32.8 | 52.0 | 0.982 |
| Single direction (MGTD head / independent probe / random) | 84.8–86.2 | — | — | ≈ 0.98 |
| INLP subspace ($k = 16$) | 89.8 | — | — | 0.974 |
| **LEACE (complete source erasure)** | 68.7 | 49.5 | 19.1 | **0.589** |
| Random whitened ×10 (placebo) | 84.9 | 32.7 | — | 0.982 |
| **srcORTH-PC1 (high variance, orthogonal to source)** | **66.7** | 54.1 | 12.6 | **0.982** |

Interpretation: single-direction and low-dimensional INLP erasure are ineffective. LEACE initially appears effective (source is indeed erased, the gap falls from 52 to 19, and the placebos are all ineffective). However, erasing a high-variance direction orthogonal to source (leaving source information entirely intact) likewise reduces the false-positive rate to 66.7 and the gap to 12.6, an effect almost identical to that of LEACE.

**E.5 Supplementary observation.** The mean false-positive rate across the two non-sarcastic source sides is almost unchanged before and after the intervention (58.8 → 59.1). The intervention does not “fix” the model; it merely transfers part of the overprediction of sarcasm on the AI side to the human side.
